## Required design

### Shared consumer identity and atomic completion
1. Use a stable consumer key plus persisted eventId as the durable uniqueness boundary. The consumer key must not change merely because code is redeployed. Keep separate receipts for different consumers of the same event. Validate the existing envelope before taking an effect; a reused identity with a conflicting envelope must fail closed.
2. Route legacy and future BullMQ invocations of each implemented consumer through the same idempotency boundary. Do not create unrelated legacy and queue receipt identities.
3. When the consumer effect and inbox are in the same database, commit the effect/intended notification rows and inbox completion in the same transaction. A failed effect leaves no completed receipt; rollback preserves retryability.
4. Prove concurrency safety with DB uniqueness/locking or bounded claims and fencing as needed. An expired worker must not overwrite a newer owner or commit an unfenced effect. Keep network/provider calls outside DB transactions.
5. A completed receipt suppresses duplicate DB effects, but must not suppress recovery of a separately persisted notification dispatch intent. Do not treat BullMQ job retention/deduplication as the permanent consumer ledger.
6. Preserve current audit ordering and public response semantics. Repeated delivery must not duplicate logical business/audit effects or hide a failed first attempt. Record any necessary, bounded recovery behavior change in USER_IMPACT_LOG.md.

### Notification-row recovery
1. Retain the existing event/channel/recipient uniqueness semantics; use the persisted Notification ID as the dispatch identity across enqueue retries, worker restart and removed queue jobs.
2. Repair the existing case where a row is committed but Queue.add fails, and a later duplicate insert returns inserted=false. That existing row must remain discoverable and recoverable without creating a second notification.
3. Select only eligible pending dispatch work, in bounded batches with safe concurrent ownership. Enqueue outside the transaction. Queue.add success followed by failed DB bookkeeping or process termination remains retryable with the same identity.
4. Keep durable intent, queue acceptance, in-flight sending, sent and uncertain provider outcome semantically distinct. Never mark sent because a job was queued. A stale worker cannot finalize a newer send claim.
5. Recheck durable delivery state before invoking any sender. Removing/completing a BullMQ job must not permit a previously sent notification to be sent again merely because queue deduplication expired.
6. Provider accept-then-timeout cannot be solved by a DB inbox. Use deterministic fake transports to prove an explicit uncertainty state/blocked recovery path, or provider idempotency when a supported contract exists. No blind retry of ambiguous real sends and no exactly-once external-delivery claim. All real providers remain BLOCKED_PROVIDER.

### Search consumer
Use deterministic projection identity and a retry-safe upsert/delete or equivalent operation for currently supported effects. Inbox completion must reflect successful projection work; a partial failure remains recoverable. If effects are outside the inbox database, demonstrate idempotent replay instead of claiming cross-system atomicity. Preserve current lifecycle/business decisions; document uncovered archive/owner behavior rather than silently expanding it.

## Historical reconciliation and future cutover
A new empty inbox cannot show whether legacy notification/search effects already occurred. Historical held records may include successful, partially successful or failed legacy dispatch, including the two events from one payment approval.

1. Inventory held records by event ID, consumer, hold reason and age using restricted read-only queries that omit payload/recipient PII. Map only verifiable existing effects, such as stable event-linked notification rows and their durable state.
2. Classify each event/consumer separately: proven completed effect, proven recoverable durable intent, proven missing effect, or uncertain/unsupported. Missing inbox/job alone is not evidence of a missing effect; absence of a search projection or an external response is not proof of no prior execution.
3. Do not seed inbox completion, mark an outbox row delivered, or promote eligibility from a guess. Proposed backfill/repair must cite effect evidence and preserve immutable original event IDs. Uncertain outcomes remain held for explicit reconciliation.
4. For mixed consumer outcomes, complete consumer-level reconciliation first. Event-level eligibility is allowed only when every required consumer has a safe disposition. One notification receipt cannot prove another consumer ran. If future reconciliation imports proven receipts and enables remaining delivery, commit those records and eligibility together; never promote first. Preserve the evidence and distinguish reconciled history from newly executed effects.
5. Keep a persisted cutover boundary/routing decision per new event when future activation is approved. A rolling deployment, restart or toggle must not route one event through both legacy and durable paths or promote older held rows. Shared inboxes remain defense in depth. Do not add automatic legacy fallback after durable enqueue failure. Choose a controlled deployment boundary or a proven persisted routing generation; process-clock time alone is not a safe boundary.
6. Produce a concrete cutover and rollback checklist: handler coverage, historical classification, notification recovery, test evidence, provider restrictions, rollout version boundary, stop criteria and preservation of durable history. Production activation is not part of this run.
7. Demonstrate this classification and replay policy on synthetic fixtures only. No production backfill, broad eligibility UPDATE, automatic cleanup or test-only DROP against a backlog-bearing database.

## Exact acceptance tests
1. Sequential and concurrent duplicate delivery, including one legacy and one durable invocation, yields one logical DB effect and one completed receipt for each implemented consumer.
2. Distinct consumer keys both process the same event; distinct events with otherwise identical payloads are not incorrectly collapsed.
3. Crash/failure before effect, after effect but before completion, and after completion is recoverable without lost intent or duplicate DB effects. Include stale-claim fencing if leases are used.
4. A notification DB insert followed by a real Redis enqueue failure leaves recoverable intent; later recovery enqueues the existing row with its original notification ID.
5. Two recovery workers and crash-after-enqueue/before-bookkeeping do not create a second notification or incorrectly mark it sent. Test queue-job removal/retention expiry against the durable sent guard.
6. A completed consumer inbox does not strand a pending notification. Sent, otherwise ineligible and uncertain outcomes follow their explicit recovery policy.
7. A fake provider accepts then times out: record uncertainty without reporting delivery success or blindly sending again. Production providers remain blocked; test doubles are labeled.
8. Search duplicate/concurrent delivery and partial failure preserve a correct projection and retryable completion semantics for implemented consumers.
9. Historical fixtures cover prior successful legacy effect, missing durable intent, partial multi-consumer execution and uncertainty. None become eligible on startup, restart, a default/flag change or inbox introduction alone.
10. Synthetic durable-only events reach real registered handlers through isolated BullMQ. Unknown, unsupported and unimplemented handler cases still fail closed without successful discard.
11. Existing order creation/cancellation, payment approval/rejection/expiry, webhook replay and concurrent cancellation/payment behavior remain unchanged. The same persisted event IDs survive all paths.
12. Logs and reconciliation summaries expose identifiers/safe categories, never recipient payloads, provider responses, tokens or secrets.
13. Any schema addition has additive rollout and forward-fix/preserve-data rollback guidance. Test forward/rollback/forward only on a newly created, guarded disposable database.
